# CCG semantic parser experiments · revised implementation plan

Start with one sentence that has two meanings. Then measure how often the grammar produces a useful answer on unseen inputs. Spend time on MORK/MM2, PeTTa, and CeTTa only after the Python reference and a native CPU parser have demonstrated useful coverage and the distilled neural baseline has established a comparison point.

The primary result is the **fraction of all requests that receive a semantically correct, accepted answer**, together with the latency and lifetime cost at that operating point. The runtime experiment measures the cost of executing the same licensed deductions on each backend.

This revision uses *Distilling Semantic Parsing into a CCG Rule System*, v1.1, 18 November 2026, SHA-256 `ce8f47176d1bdfaca3b63dfde6850838f00ac330027c2cd3766ce3c1141e1b56`. Milestone numbers refer to Appendix A. The scope spike and the native, neural, and existing-symbolic baseline milestones are additions. All counts, budgets, staffing assignments, and thresholds below are proposals for M01 sign-off. All results are still to be measured. ## Start here · one sentence before the contract freeze

Time-box a disposable Python spike to two engineering days. Parse **Every researcher reviewed a paper** from tokens. Map *reviewed* to the lemma *review*, the `Review` event frame, and a past-tense feature. Give the verb two licensed scope recipes at the same transitive category. Combine *every researcher* and *a paper* into noun phrases, apply the verb to the object, then combine the subject with the resulting verb phrase. Keep both recipes reachable from one packed root.

The spike passes when it emits two replayable derivations and two distinct normalized meanings. In a world with two researchers, each reviewing a different paper and neither reviewing the other's paper, “each researcher reviewed some paper” must be true and “one paper was reviewed by every researcher” must be false. Rerun with the lexical insertion order reversed and require the same meanings and structural tie order. Save the input, output, model, and any contract ambiguity for M01. If the spike fails, resolve the scope or packing discrepancy before scaling the implementation.

### Data and adjudication budget

Plan for **6,000 controlled inputs** in 4,000 training, 1,000 development, 500 calibration, and 500 locked-test slots. Group by document or scenario, paraphrase family, semantic template, and entity-substitution family before assigning splits. Have D generate and annotate the locked portion inside the private evaluator, including its teacher cache. Reserve some combinations of known constructions for transfer tests. Audit exact and near-duplicate leakage and group overlap. Select the 300 natural development sentences separately by source document. Withhold a further natural test only if the project proposes a natural-text release claim.

Budget 6,000 initial teacher annotations plus 200 repeat calls on a prespecified sample to measure instability. Run a 100-input pricing and schema pilot within that allowance. Record actual tokens, cost, rejects, and conflicts, then obtain a monetary ceiling before releasing the remaining batches. Cache raw replies, validated target sets, preferred readings, and optional derivation hints separately. An invalid reply consumes its slot and cost. Record missing targets and their effect on every comparison.

Budget human review for 200 controlled development inputs, 100 natural development inputs, and all 500 locked controlled inputs. Select cases before seeing parser decisions, retaining ambiguous and rejected cases. A second reader independently scores 20% of each sample and disagreements go to adjudication. At a planning allowance of five minutes per reading, 960 readings require about 80 reader-hours plus reconciliation. Track actual time in the pilot. For a 98% accepted-accuracy claim, even 149 independent accepted cases with zero errors only just reach a one-sided 95% exact lower bound of 98%. Use family-aware intervals when cases share a source. Publish an inconclusive bound when the evidence falls short.

## What the parser and evaluator share

- **Grammar and semantics.** Use a finite typed CCG with forward/backward application, degree-one harmonic composition, adjacent nonempty spans, and certified acyclic unary conversions. The DSL checker enforces category and feature bounds and rejects unlicensed raising or side effects. Noun phrases denote generalized quantifiers. Clauses accept a head-event continuation. Open a quantified controller once, share its bound reference with the embedded role, then close the clause at its licensed boundary.
- **Normalization and storage.** Keep raw and normalized typed formulas plus a transformation trace. Use capture-safe binding, beta reduction, and only the declared scoped conjunction identities. Preserve quantifier order, role direction, repeated conjuncts, negation, and intensional boundaries. Store formulas as inert tagged data, including in a reasoning space. An unboxed query must retrieve zero asserted leaving events from either “Alice did not leave” or “Alice promised Bob to leave” in an otherwise empty store. Version learned SENF consolidations and background inferences separately.
- **Exact search.** A packed node includes the sentence, span, category and features, and everything a future guard or local score can inspect. Distinct edges preserve ordered children, lexical/rule identity, typed recipe, provenance, and local cost. Reinsertions of the same edge are idempotent. Finish exhaustive forest construction before ranking by additive fixed-point cost and structural ties. Emit witnesses, raw meanings, normalized meanings, and input, bundle, and policy digests.
- **Ambiguity.** Deduplicate by normalized meaning before deciding acceptance. Two lexical scope derivations for “Alice reviewed Bob” can yield one meaning. For genuinely different scope readings with no disambiguating input, default to `ambiguous` and return both with witnesses. A per-verb cost can order them for display. Only a declared, independently calibrated feature may justify resolving this ambiguity. Ambiguous requests count in the total population and contribute zero single-answer accepted coverage.
- **Two status axes.** Record construction as `complete`, `truncated`, `no_parse`, or `error`, and the decision as `accepted`, `ambiguous`, or `abstain`. Only exhaustion with no licensed root justifies `no_parse`. A timeout records partial-search status, even if a root exists. Use abstention on truncated search in the initial policy. Unsupported operations yield a named error and abstention. External fallback has a separately measured decision, result, latency, and cost.

### How to compare meanings

1. **Exact normalized teacher agreement.** Test membership in the validated set of licensed teacher targets under the pinned ontology and normalization version. Score a designated preference separately. Report agreement among comparable outputs and show their count relative to all inputs.
2. **Agreement on finite models.** For the supported fragment, evaluate the candidate and each target over the same frozen bank of typed worlds and assignments. Record whether any one target has the same entire truth vector. Include role swaps, scope-separating worlds, a nonempty negation countermodel, and a pair that moves independent existentials while preserving truth. A separating world is a reproducible semantic counterexample. A matching vector supports agreement on those worlds only. Mark unsupported intensional phenomena as unscored and publish the eligible denominator. Have the independent reader verify the contrast worlds ahead of evaluation.
3. **Adjudicated utility.** Blind readers to system identity. Score roles, scope, controller identity, event attachment, and acceptable ambiguity using the input and permitted context. Report accepted coverage (accepted/all), accepted accuracy (correct/accepted), correct yield (correct and accepted/all), and counts on both status axes. Accepted accuracy is undefined with zero acceptances. Include per-family results and a fixed role/scope query task. Inspect exact mismatches with matching truth vectors for notation differences before proposing a lexical patch.

## Workstream 1 · can the grammar earn its coverage?

The dependency order is spike → M01 → M02–M08 → bounded growth (M13) → primary comparison (M14/M15). The native, neural, and existing-symbolic baselines have their own deliverables. They can overlap grammar work once their inputs are frozen. M09–M12 are outside this dependency chain.

**Decision 1 — after the seed.** Continue to grammar growth only with all core contrasts passing, both scope readings recoverable, and zero unexplained tiny-oracle mismatches. A missing target in the forest calls for a lexical, composition, or packing diagnosis. A target present but ranked low calls for selection work. A pruned target calls for a search-budget diagnosis.

**Decision 2 — after growth and the primary development comparison.** Open the runtime workstream only if (i) at least 600 of the 1,000 controlled development inputs receive complete, accepted symbolic answers, (ii) at least 100 of the 200 audited controlled inputs are accepted and at least 95% of those are adjudicated correct, (iii) the native implementation passes reference parity and the registered CPU serving budget, and (iv) the matched neural comparison has been measured. These development thresholds decide whether to invest in adapters. They carry no claim about natural-text performance. Freeze and report the primary candidate even if the gate fails. If missing context dominates, propose a separately versioned context/fallback experiment. If the neural system dominates in audited quality, coverage, latency, and lifetime cost at the target load, end adapter investment and publish that result.

**M14/M15 primary evaluation.** Benchmark the reference, native parser, neural model, teacher, and existing symbolic pipeline where supported. Fit acceptance policies on the 500 calibration inputs and freeze all systems before D runs the 500 locked cases once. A controlled-domain release qualifies if it reaches ≥60% accepted coverage, the one-sided 95% lower bound for adjudicated accepted accuracy reaches ≥95%, and the native parser meets its registered serving budget. Publish all 500 outcomes and uncertainty even on failure. Grammar and model authors receive aggregate locked results after freeze. Opening an adapter profile never delays this evaluation.

## Measurement, cost, and release

- **Observations.** Use at least five randomized repetition blocks per timed local configuration. Key each row by configuration, workload/input, repetition, and cold/warm condition. Record unsupported, interrupted, or errored attempts with a reason and an unavailable timing where appropriate. Measure full online latency (p50/p95/p99), throughput, CPU-seconds, peak process-group memory, startup and load, active alternatives, and forest sizes. Include host work and transfer. Report counts for the full population and the paired common-completion subset. Cache teacher responses for fidelity evaluation and budget live service latency samples separately.
- **Ablations.** Hold workload, ontology, and evaluator fixed. Vary lexical inventory at fixed grammar, templates at fixed lexicon, composition, selection costs, ambiguity pruning, and normalization. For runtime profiles compare native joins and moving one stage across the host boundary. Report changes in target reachability, selected quality, accepted coverage, correct yield, and latency. Disable any learned consolidation separately.
- **Break-even.** Compare at matched quality and coverage. With incremental build cost B, symbolic serving cost S per request, fallback rate f, cost F conditional on fallback, and teacher-only serving cost T, the routed system costs B + N(S + fF) for N requests. It recovers a positive build investment when T > S + fF and N > B/(T − S − fF). For the neural comparison, use separate fixed costs for grammar development and model training and each system’s measured per-request cost. Count shared annotations once on each side. Preregister plausible request volumes and report sensitivity to refresh frequency, human labor, and fallback rate.
- **M16 and replay.** Package manifests, input and configuration digests, code and grammar versions, ontology, policy, gates, raw evidence, and support matrix. In a clean environment reproduce one complete and one failing or abstained example per claimed profile and one primary comparison report. Interrupt and resume one annotation batch and one benchmark block with zero duplicate committed observations. Renew capability checks after runtime changes and recalibrate after a teacher change.
- **Effort and cuts.** Budget roughly 2 days for the spike, 2 weeks for contract/semantics/data setup, 3 weeks for seed grammar and reference/oracle, up to 4 weeks for growth, 2–3 weeks for the native and neural baselines, and 1–2 weeks for calibration, evaluation, and release. Adjudication adds about 80 reader-hours plus reconciliation to these engineering estimates. Allow another 1–2 weeks for common IR and up to 30 engineering days for three runtime qualification attempts if Decision 2 passes. Under time pressure, cut runtime optimization and further grammar expansion first. Keep the held-out comparison, failure counts, and reproducible fixtures in the primary release.
